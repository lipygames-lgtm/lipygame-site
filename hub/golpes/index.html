Safety guide

How to spot mobile game scams before they take your account

LHBy Lipy Hub Team·Updated September 28
Quick answer

If it asks for your password, promises infinite items, or tells you to download an app outside the official store, it's a scam. Real codes only come from the game's official channels.

Code and diamond generators don't exist

Every Free Fire code is created by Garena and announced on the game's official channels. No site, app, or video can generate a valid code, and none can put free diamonds in your account.

What these generators actually do is ask for your login to "deliver" the items, or make you go through a "human verification" that ends in a sign-up, an app download, or a paid survey. In the end, the scammer wins: they get your account, your data, or ad money.

Free Robux is a scam too

On Roblox, Robux only reach your account through in-app purchases, a Premium subscription, or an official gift card. Sites that promise Robux in exchange for your login, a download, or tasks are traps to steal accounts.

The same goes for anyone offering rare fruits in Blox Fruits, limited items, or "admin" in exchange for your password. Real trades happen inside the game, with both players accepting on screen.

Modded APKs and hacks: the price is your account

Modified versions of games, downloaded outside Google Play or the App Store, can carry viruses and almost always get your account banned. Games detect altered files, and the ban is usually permanent.

That's why Lipy Hub doesn't publish links to modded APKs, hacks, or cheats for online play. We only include what you can use without putting your account at risk.

Warning signs

  • It asks for your password, the code you get by text message, or your social media login.
  • It promises "infinite" or "unlimited" diamonds, Robux, skins, or items.
  • It rushes you: "today only," "only a few spots left," "the code expires in 5 minutes."
  • It tells you to download an app, an extension, or an APK to "unlock" the prize.
  • It asks for a "human verification," a survey, or a sign-up before handing anything over.
  • The site's address looks like the official one, but has swapped or extra letters.

I got scammed. Now what?

  1. Change your password right awayStart with the account linked to the game (Google, Facebook, Apple, X), then the game's own account, if it has one.
  2. Turn on two-step verificationWith it, even someone who has your password can't get in without the code from your phone.
  3. Remove access for unfamiliar appsIn your Google or Facebook account settings, remove apps and sites you don't recognize.
  4. Contact the game's official supportOnly use in-game support or the official website. Be wary of "account recovery" offers on social media.
